# Business
Wise is interesting because it operates a technology-led international payments and money-management platform addressing the friction and cost associated with moving money across borders. Its products span international transfers, multi-currency accounts, spending and business services. Wise reported FY2026 net revenue of approximately $2.50bn, up 19% year-on-year.

# Financial Performance

Financial performance has shown strong revenue growth, although profitability has been less consistent. FY2026 revenue reached $2,502.8m, while operating income was $590.7m and net income was $498.7m. The model forecasts revenue increasing to approximately $2,940.8m in FY2027F, $3,440.7m in FY2028F and $3,991.2m in FY2029F.

# Valuation

The DCF and comparable-company approaches provide different perspectives on Wise's value. The DCF is particularly sensitive to WACC, terminal growth and forecast free cash flow, while the comparable approach depends heavily on the multiples observed across the selected peer group. The peer analysis produces a median EV/Revenue multiple of approximately 2.01x and a median P/E of approximately 14.46x.

# Risks

Competition: Wise operates in a competitive international payments and fintech market.
Regulation: Payments and money-transfer businesses face extensive regulatory and compliance requirements.
FX exposure: Changes in exchange rates can affect reported financial performance and transaction economics.
Margin pressure: Maintaining rapid revenue growth while controlling operating costs will be important as Wise becomes larger.
Technology/cyber risk: Wise depends heavily on technology infrastructure, security and service availability.
Customer growth: Slower customer acquisition or transaction growth could reduce the growth assumptions underpinning the valuation.

# Final Investment View

The analysis indicates that Wise has attractive growth characteristics, supported by continued expansion in international money movement, increasing customer adoption and the scalability of its technology-led platform. Revenue growth and the company's ability to convert growth into sustainable operating profit are important strengths in the valuation case.

However, the valuation remains sensitive to the assumptions used in both the DCF and comparable-company analysis. In particular, WACC, terminal growth, future customer expansion and the level of sustainable margins can materially affect the implied value.

The comparable analysis also produces a wide valuation range because different peers have different growth rates, profitability profiles and business models. This highlights the importance of considering valuation multiples alongside the underlying financial characteristics of each company.

Further investigation should focus on Wise's sustainable long-term growth rate, operating-margin trajectory, customer growth, regulatory environment and the treatment of customer balances when moving from enterprise value to equity value.
